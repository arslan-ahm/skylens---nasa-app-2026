05 — How it works

How does the phone know where you are pointing?

A gravity sensor feels which way is down, so it knows how high you are aiming.

A compass feels the Earth's magnetic north, so it knows which way you face.

Compasses wander near metal. Line up the Moon once and SkyLens corrects itself.
The second question: how does the phone know where you are pointing it? Every smartphone has two small sensors that do this. A gravity sensor feels which way is down, so the phone knows how steeply you are tilting it, which is how high in the sky you are aiming. A compass feels the Earth's magnetic field, so the phone knows which direction you are facing. Put the two together and you get an exact direction in the sky, which SkyLens matches with the sky calculator from the last slide. One honest catch: phone compasses get confused near metal, cars or magnetic phone cases. So SkyLens lets you line up the Moon or a bright planet once, tap calibrate, and it corrects itself.
