The Earth's spin turns the sky about 15° every hour, so SkyLens re-does the sums every second, even with no internet.

Now, how does it actually work? No magic, just very well-known maths. SkyLens combines three things. Your place, from the phone's location. The exact time, from the phone's clock. And a catalogue stored on the phone: 2,865 stars bright enough to see, the 88 constellations, and the known paths of the Moon and planets. A small sky calculator on the phone mixes those together and works out where everything sits in your sky right now. Because the Earth spins, the sky turns about fifteen degrees every hour, so the app recalculates every second. All of this happens on the phone, so it works in the middle of a field with no signal.
